Portfolio purpose

A record of development, not only completed products

I entered AIL 601 with practical beliefs formed through more than ten years of teaching mathematics and physics, academic leadership, and distance education. I already valued active participation, conceptual understanding, practice, collaboration, feedback, and access.

The course helped me identify the theories behind those beliefs, question assumptions, and develop clearer rules for selecting, implementing, and evaluating technology. The philosophy and three Cool Resources show how my thinking became more explicit and evidence-centered.

Evidence of learning

How the artifacts show my growth

Each stage added a question that now guides my instructional decisions.

01

How does learning happen?

I connected my classroom practices with behaviorist, cognitivist, constructivist, sociocultural, motivational, and online learning perspectives.

02

What design best fits the problem?

I used TPACK, SAMR, and UDL to connect content, pedagogy, technology, access, and evidence in the same instructional context.

03

What will sustain meaningful use?

I used UTAUT and related adoption models to separate acceptance, implementation, continued use, and improved learning.

Portfolio narrative

How my thinking developed

Already present

Learning and intentionality

I already believed in prior knowledge, active participation, conceptual understanding, guided practice, collaboration, feedback, learner independence, and using technology for a clear instructional reason.

Added

Framework-informed design

I added explicit attention to cognitive load, UDL, the relationship among content, pedagogy and technology, responsible AI, adoption conditions, accessibility, and implementation support.

Modified

What counts as success

I no longer treat enjoyment, confidence, repeated use, or technological sophistication as sufficient evidence. I separate them from whether learners can explain, apply, demonstrate, and transfer understanding.

My enduring lesson

My philosophy has moved from a general belief in intentional technology use to a clearer process connecting need → theory → strategy → technology → evidence → revision. Rejecting or discontinuing an unsuitable tool can be as instructionally sound as adopting a new one.
