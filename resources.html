Cool Resource 1

OER remix · UDL · Responsible AI

Crop Resilience Challenge

I remixed openly licensed content about crop adaptation and drought resilience into an interactive decision-making experience. Images, narration, sentence frames, planning choices, and revision opportunities reduce barriers while maintaining a common learning goal.

Design problem
Help learners connect evidence about drought adaptation with a defensible crop plan.
Theory-to-feature connection
Constructivist problem solving shaped the crop-planning task. UDL shaped the multiple representations and response supports.
Revision evidence
Peer feedback led me to correct the number of guiding ideas, clarify benefits and trade-offs, and add a conclusion that supports completion or iteration.
What I learned
AI verification, accessibility, theory alignment, and Creative Commons obligations belong inside the design process, not after it.

Cool Resource 2

TPACK · SAMR · UDL

Boyle's Law Design Lab

This resource addresses an abstract science relationship: at constant temperature, decreasing gas volume increases pressure. Learners manipulate values, observe particle behavior, collect evidence, and explain the inverse relationship using written, visual, or audio responses.

Design problem
Make an invisible particle mechanism understandable to beginning science learners.
Framework comparison
TPACK connects the physics content, inquiry strategy, and simulation. SAMR explains how dynamic investigation modifies the task. UDL provides equivalent routes to perceive information and explain understanding.
Revision evidence
Instructor feedback led me to make constant temperature explicit and add a transfer check scored with common success criteria across response modes.
What I learned
The highest SAMR level is not automatically best. The appropriate level is the one that supports the learning problem without adding unnecessary complexity.

Cool Resource 3

UTAUT · Implementation support · Generative AI

Purposeful AI Readiness Lab

This diagnostic resource helps Nigerian secondary-school teachers and leaders examine readiness for responsible generative AI use. It keeps the four UTAUT profiles distinct, then translates an identified barrier into a feasible support action.

Design problem
Move beyond asking whether teachers like AI toward diagnosing what would support meaningful and sustained use.
Model-to-feature connection
Performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions organize the readiness check and support roadmap.
Revision evidence
Peer feedback led me to add targeted next steps, separate electricity, devices, connectivity, and data affordability, and provide a printable group version for low-connectivity settings.
What I learned
Acceptance, implementation, continuance, and improved learning are different outcomes. A low profile should begin a support conversation, not label a teacher.
